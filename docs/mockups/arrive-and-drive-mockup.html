What’s included

What you get, and what you bring

One weekend, one car, everything around it. Nothing on this list is an add-on.

  • ✓The car

    A race-ready Spec Racer Ford Gen3, sealed and equal to every other car on the grid. Prepped before every event: alignment, nut and bolt, wash, driver fitment.

  • ✓Transport

    Truck, trailer and crew to the track and home again. You never touch a trailer. Transport is shared by the cars aboard, so a full trailer costs you less.

  • ✓Trackside crew

    Mechanical checks in the morning and after every session, tire pressures in pit lane, and mechanics in the paddock and on pit wall if something happens.

  • ✓Radio

    We put you on our radios, so you hear flag status, time left in the session and where you are on track. Agreed with you before the session, not improvised during it.

  • ✓Coaching

    Session-specific goals before you go out. Video and data after every session, reviewed with you in plain language: the car, your driving, your race craft.

  • ✓Fuel and tires

    Estimated up front, settled on what you actually use. One session is one heat cycle, and you pay for the cycles you consume.

  • ✓The awning

    Shade, seats and something to drink for you and whoever you bring. Spouses, kids, friends, a client or two.

  • —You bring

    Helmet, suit, gloves, shoes, arm restraints and a head-and-neck restraint, your travel, and your entry fee with the organizer. No arm restraints or head-and-neck restraint yet? We can provide them, and renting a full set of gear is coming.

The day

What a test day actually looks like

Times are from a Chin Track Days weekend at Road Atlanta, advanced solo group. Test days at Polecat run more open track; race weekends add qualifying and the races. The loop is the same everywhere.

  • 7:30inferred

    You arrive. The car is off the trailer, checked and warmed through. You sign in with the organizer and sit in on the drivers’ meeting.

  • Before session one

    The walkaround. Belts and seat, the start-up ritual, the sequential gearbox, what the dash is telling you, how we talk on the radio. You do it, we watch. It is all in the binder in the trailer if you want to read ahead.

  • 9:00

    Session one, twenty minutes. Out-lap to put temperature in the car, then laps. We are on the radio with flags and time remaining.

  • After every session

    Pressures, a nut-and-bolt look, then the data. Video and data with a coach, in plain language. One thing to try next time out.

  • Noon · 1:30 · 3:00

    Sessions two to four, same loop. Between sessions the awning is yours: shade, seats, something to drink, your people.

  • End of day

    The car goes back on the trailer. You go home with the video, and we settle tires and fuel on what you actually used.

Three questions everyone asks

Do I need a racing license?

Not for a test day or a track day. Time trials need a Time Trials license, a quick online application with no racing school. Wheel-to-wheel racing needs an SCCA competition license or novice permit. The calendar marks every event.

License, on the FAQ

What happens if I crash?

The $6,000 crash-damage deposit covers repairs and comes back in full if the car is undamaged. Normal wear is ours, not yours. The full policy is on the FAQ.legal review
