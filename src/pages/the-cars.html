The car itself

Spec Racer Ford Gen3, from SportsCar Club of America

The Spec Racer Ford Gen3 is a high-performance, open cockpit, closed-wheel racecar for paved road courses manufactured and marketed by SCCA Enterprises. The racer is powered by a 135-horsepower, 1.6-liter, four-cylinder engine and features suspension adjustments within described parameters. SCCA Enterprises is a subsidiary of SCCA, Inc., and the Spec Racer Ford is the ultimate spec competition. All major components are sealed and monitored for compliance at the race events. The SRF is quick and reliable, and it is one of SCCA racing’s largest groups.

The Spec Racer Ford is one of the largest groups in SCCA racing. Full grids, close racing, and a real race with somebody to dice with from green to checker.

A purpose-built racing car

Not a road car with a cage in it. Open cockpit, closed wheels, built by SCCA Enterprises for paved road courses: 135 horsepower from a 1.6-litre four, a sequential gearbox, no driver aids, and you sitting low enough to feel the surface change.

It is quick enough to teach you everything that matters and forgiving enough to let you learn it. That combination is rarer than it sounds, and it is why this car is one of the most accessible ways into real road racing in the country.

The handover

You will know this car before you drive it.

Nobody hands you a key and points at the track. Before your first session one of us walks you through the car. You do it, we watch. Every bit of it is written down in the SRF3 Driver Operations & Communication Manual, a tabbed binder that lives in the trailer and is yours for the weekend.

Here is some of what you will pick up.

The moment it lights

Master switch, a wait while the sensor warms, a pulse of the starter, and then half the paddock looks up. It is a ritual more than a procedure, and the first morning you run through it without being prompted is the morning the car starts feeling like yours.

Out-lap, grid, green flag

What actually happens between firing it up and going racing: when to roll, how to put temperature into the car on the out-lap, the way grid forms up, and what the flags are telling you. Somebody walks it through with you, first weekend or fiftieth.

The sequential gearbox

Quick, decisive and unlike anything you have driven on the road. Being gentle with it is the one instinct you have to unlearn. We will have you shifting it properly before you leave the paddock.

What every light on the dash means

Which colour means keep going and tell us, which means come in now, and the one that means pit immediately and do not argue. Plus the numbers the car wants to live between.

How we talk on the radio

What to tell us, what we will feed you, and when you actually need to answer. Agreed with you before the session rather than improvised during it.

What the data says about your driving

The car logs brake pressure, steering, throttle, G and GPS, and films it. You go home with the video. We go through it with you in plain language, not as a spreadsheet.

Read the binder once before your first weekend and there is a one-page reference in the back to scan on grid. Anything it does not answer, ask. Questions are the point, not an interruption.
