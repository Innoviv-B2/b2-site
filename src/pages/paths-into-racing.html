Getting started

Four ways into racing, costed end to end

Nobody is ready for a Hoosier Super Tour the Monday after a license school. A license is a credential; speed and racecraft are hours. These are two different clocks, and almost all of the money follows the second one. Here is what each route actually costs, what you are holding at the end of it, and how to pick.

Every figure below is dated, sourced and reproducible. Where a competitor does not publish a number, we say so rather than estimating one.

The two clocks

The license is the cheap part

The cheapest legal route to a Full Competition License in the United States runs about $5,400 all-in: MSR Houston's three-day school in their Spec Racer Ford, plus the drive from Huntsville, plus SCCA membership and the license fee. We rent Spec Racer Fords and we will still point you there if the license is all you want — we wrote the whole comparison up on the license page.

But look at what you are holding when you walk out: a license, a school certificate, and somewhere between three and ten hours of track time — in a car you will probably never sit in again, 1,542 miles from home.

The Hoosier Super Tour at Barber costs $795 to enter, requires a Full license, and has no test day. The gap between those two paragraphs is what this page is about — it is also, in plain terms, how much does it cost to start racing versus how much does it cost to be ready to race.

The readiness ladder

Where you actually are, and what's next

Licensing is a ladder with two rungs. Getting good is a ladder with six, and it keeps going past the top of club racing. This is the one that matters, and the useful thing about it is that you can start on it today, with no license, no school and no medical form.

  • Rung 0

    Track Night in America, open lapping. Needs nothing. It's for finding out whether you like this at all. Our crew, if it's our car.

  • Rung 1

    Talladega GP Time Attack, two days. Needs SCCA membership and your driver's license. It's for your first timed laps — no wheel-to-wheel, no pressure, a real clock. Crew and car; coaching by arrangement.

  • Rung 2

    An accredited drivers' school. Needs membership only, since September 2025. It's for the credential. The school's own instructors.

  • Rung 3

    Regional SARRC weekends — Buccaneer at Roebling, Barber Double. Needs a Novice Permit, school signed off. It's for your first races, small fields, low stakes. Crew and car. Cliff is at the track.

  • Rung 4

    SEDIV Majors, Roebling. Needs a Full Competition License. It's for real pace, real fields. Crew and car. Cliff is at the track.

  • Rung 5

    Barber Hoosier Super Tour. Needs a Full license — no test day. It's the thing you thought you were ready for. Crew and car. Cliff is at the track.

  • Rung 6

    The SCCA National Championship Runoffs. Needs a Full license and a qualifying national season. It's the top of club racing: one race per class, a national title on the line.   • Rung 7

    To infinity and beyond: pro racing. Formula 4, GT World Challenge and GT America, Trans Am, MX-5 Cup. Needs a pro license from the series, a results record and a budget of a different order. It's where club racing hands you off. We will help you build the résumé and the data to get there. needs Cliff: which pro series to name, and what B2 actually offers at this rung

Rungs 1, 3, 4 and 5 are all track time — and track time is the thing that is actually scarce. Rung 2 is the paperwork. Most people plan the paperwork and improvise the rest.

The honest trade

The schools are cheaper. Here's what the difference buys.

PoleCat, MSR Houston and Hallett will all put you in a car for less than it costs to rent ours and tow it to a track. That is not close and we are not going to pretend otherwise — the arithmetic is two sections up. So the only question that matters is what the difference is for.

One car, all season

A school puts you in their car for two or three days. Everything you learn about that car — how it takes a curb, where it gives up, what it does on old tires — stops being useful the moment you climb out. Our SRF3 is the same car in February and August. Nothing resets.

The same crew, who know that car

Not a rotating shop and not you in a paddock with a toolbox. The people who set the car up at Roebling are the people who set it up at Barber, and they remember what you asked for last time. When a customer put a rear corner into the wall at Mid-Ohio on a wet Saturday, the car was repaired and on grid Sunday morning.

Cliff is at race weekends

He isn't dropping a car off. He's running the weekend, which means the debrief between sessions is part of it — from someone who has won a national championship in this car, three times. Track days and time trials run a little differently: those go out with the crew and the car, and coaching there is arranged in advance. No truck, no trailer, no prep weekend

The car is transported, prepped, fuelled, run and put away. You drive to the track. For anyone with a job, this is frequently the actual deciding factor, not the money.

The costs nobody quotes you

Four of these are ours to be straight about, and one is an industry problem.

Damage

The single biggest unpublished cost in this category

Almost no racing school publishes its damage terms at all — of everyone we surveyed, most say only that the student is "responsible for damages" and leave it there. Lucas Oil caps a driver at $8,000 for a first incident and never states what a second one costs. Ours: a $6,000 crash-damage deposit, returned in full if the car comes back undamaged needs Cliff: confirm the $6,000 deposit still stands. Stated on the pricing page, where it has always been.

Safety gear

$1,000–1,600 for a legal minimum kit

Suit, helmet, head-and-neck restraint, arm restraints, shoes, gloves — more to buy once and buy well. We wrote the whole thing up, including what the GCR actually requires and where to buy it: buying safety gear.

SCCA fees

You'll be quoted differently everywhere

Permit and license fees are not published by SCCA in 2026. Budget about $275 for both and call 1-800-770-2055 to confirm.

Tires and fuel

Estimated up front, settled on what you use

Scuffed tires are a real option and they're what most people run — the difference across a Barber weekend is around $850.

Transport and prep

Charged once per event, not per day

Which is exactly why a single day looks expensive and a two-day weekend doesn't. Our benchmark two-day track weekend at Barber — four hours on track over eight sessions, used tires, one of three cars in the trailer — is $4,468, around $4,500.
