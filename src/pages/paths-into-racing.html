The license is the cheap part

The lowest-priced school we found that ends in a Full Competition License with a car supplied is MSR Houston: about $5,400 with membership, the license fee and travel from Huntsville. Closer to home, the two-day school at Polecat Training Center (PTC) in Lynchburg, TN is $3,625 for a Novice Permit, car included. We rent Spec Racer Fords and we will still point you to a school if the license is all you want. Every school's price is on the license page.

A license school gives you three to ten hours of track time. The Hoosier Super Tour at Barber costs $795 to enter, needs a Full license and has no test day. This page is about the distance between the two. Getting a license is the easy part. Getting good is the hard part, and it is also the fun part.

The journey

Progress comes in plateaus

Getting good in a race car does not happen in a straight line. It comes in plateaus: stretches where nothing seems to change, then a jump. Here is roughly what each one takes. You can start on the first today with SCCA membership and a driver's license.

  • Plateau 1

    At home in the car. Track days and Time Trials: a real clock, no wheel-to-wheel. Within a handful of weekends most drivers stop thinking about the car and start thinking about the track.

  • Plateau 2

    Racing, and finishing. A school and a Novice Permit put you on a regional grid. The first race weekends are about starts, traffic and bringing the car home. Most people feel settled after three to five.

  • Plateau 3

    Within a second of the fast drivers. This is pure seat time: one to two years of steady running gets most committed drivers there. The stopwatch shows it, which makes it the most satisfying jump.

  • Plateau 4

    Racecraft. Strategy, tire management, where to pass, and what to do on lap one with a full field around you. It takes longer than the speed did, and it turns a quick lap into a good result at a Major, a Super Tour or the Runoffs.

  • And on

    Your own measure. A regional championship, a national podium, or a cleaner lap at your home track than last year. Every hour in the car adds to it.

What that looks like in real results

One of our SRF3 drivers came to the sport as an adult. In late 2024 and spring 2025 he was about two seconds a lap off the fastest SRF3s. By summer 2025 he was within a second at Road Atlanta, and that June he set the SRF3 lap record at Atlanta Motorsports Park and finished third in class: plateau three arriving, with plateau four right behind it. In 2026 he took on the Hoosier Super Tour, in fields of 38 to 47 cars, and finished between 14th and 39th. His best, 14th of 40, came at Barber in August.

Another, a teenager from the Atlanta area, finished SCCA drivers' school in March 2025 and won an SRF3 race at a SEDIV Major about 15 months later, in a seven-car field. That October he ran his first Runoffs and finished 24th of 44, about five seconds a lap off the fastest laps. Quick in a small field, then a big step up against the best in the country.

The honest trade

The schools are cheaper. Here's what the difference buys.

A school day in a PTC, MSR Houston or Hallett car costs less than renting ours and towing it to a track, and the table above shows by how much.

Seat time in the car you race

A school puts you in its car for two or three days, often a formula car or a sports car you are unlikely to race. The SRF3 is a sealed spec car: one drives like another, and your seat insert moves with you to any of them. Every hour in ours, from a Monday test to a Super Tour, builds on the last.

The same crew, who know that car

The people who set the car up at Roebling set it up at Barber, and they remember what you asked for last time. When a customer put a rear corner into the wall at Mid-Ohio on a wet Saturday, the car was repaired and on grid Sunday morning.

Access to qualified coaches

Renting from us means access to our coaches, Cliff White and Mark Eaton, and to coaches in our network who know this car: Evan Slater, Morgan Burkhard and Dave Ogburn. At race weekends coaching is included: data, video and a plan for the next session, between every session. At a track day, test day or time trial, a coach is guaranteed when four or more of our cars make the trip; with fewer, a coach is available if you ask when you book. Video and data review is always available, and private coaching is $800 a day.

No truck, no trailer, no prep weekend

A school also hands you a prepared car, so against a school this one is even. Against owning your own SRF it is the whole difference: we transport, prep, fuel, run and put away the car, and you drive to the track. For anyone with a job, that often decides it more than the money does.

The costs nobody quotes you

What a sticker price leaves out, and how each one works with us.

Damage

The biggest unpublished cost in this category

Most racing schools say only that the student is "responsible for damages." Ours is in the open. Damage you cause is yours to pay for; a mechanical failure that is not your fault is not. The $6,000 crash-damage deposit is held while you race with us and returned when you're done. It is security, not a limit on what a repair can cost.

We show what that means in practice, with the common crashes we see and how each repair is billed: what a typical crash costs.

Safety gear

$1,000–1,600 for a legal minimum kit

Suit, helmet, head-and-neck restraint, arm restraints, shoes and gloves. You buy it once and it goes with you to every car and every track, so buy it well.

What the GCR requires and where to buy it: buying safety gear.

SCCA fees

Published, and small next to the driving

SCCA membership is $85 a year plus region dues. A Novice Permit is $150 and a Full Competition License is $125, both bought in SCCA's online store at my.scca.com once a school signs you off.

If anything about your case is unusual, call SCCA on 1-800-770-2055.

Tires and fuel

Estimated up front, settled on what you use

Our estimates assume scuffed Hoosiers, which is what most people run. A new set across a Barber weekend adds around $850.

You can ask for a different tire plan: new Hoosiers, used Hoosiers, or Toyo R888Rs, which last longer and lap slower.

Transport and prep

Every line is a specific job

Prep gets the car ready, aligned and fitted to you. Transport is charged once per event. The car is rented by the hour on track, and the trackside crew by the day. You come, have fun and learn along the way.

That is why a single day looks expensive and a two-day weekend doesn't. Our benchmark two-day track weekend at Barber, two cars in the trailer, is $4,533.66, around $4,500.
