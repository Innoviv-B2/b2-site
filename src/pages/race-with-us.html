What's included

What you get, and what you bring

One weekend, one car, everything around it. Nothing on this list is an add-on. A Spec Racer Ford arrive and drive weekend is this list, start to finish.

  • ✓The car

    A race-ready Spec Racer Ford Gen3, sealed and equal to every other car on the grid. Prepped before every event: alignment, nut and bolt, wash, driver fitment. More about the car

  • ✓Transport

    We get the car to the track and home again. You drive yourself to the track; the car is there when you arrive.

  • ✓Trackside crew

    Mechanical checks in the morning and after every session, tire pressures in pit lane, and mechanics in the paddock and on pit wall if something happens. Meet the crew

  • ✓Data and video

    Every rental car carries AiM data acquisition and a SmartyCam, so every session comes home as data and in-car video, reviewed in Race Studio 3. We have benchmark data for most tracks in most conditions, so even if you are at the track on your own you have a reference lap to measure against.

  • ✓Radio

    Every car has a hardwired digital radio with an IMSA-style helmet connector. Bring your helmet and earphones and you hear flag status, time left in the session and where you are on track, and you can talk to pit lane with any question you have. What we say and when is agreed with you before the session, not improvised during it.

  • ✓Coaching

    Session-specific goals before you go out. Video and data after every session, reviewed with you in plain language: the car, your driving, your race craft. You have access to Cliff, Mark and other qualified coaches; at track days and time trials, coaching is arranged in advance.

  • ✓Fuel and tires

    Estimated up front, settled on what you actually use. One session is one heat cycle, and you pay for the cycles you consume. Pick the tire that fits your budget and your goals: Toyo R88Rs last longer but lap slower, used Hoosiers are what most people run, and new Hoosiers are there when the result matters.

  • ✓The awning

    Shade and seats, and there are always snacks and water. When the schedule allows, we bring lunch. Your family and friends are welcome; we regularly bring ours too.

  • —You bring

    Helmet with an IMSA-style radio kit and earphones, suit, gloves, shoes, arm restraints and a head-and-neck restraint, your travel, and your entry fee with the organizer. The day

What a test day actually looks like

Times are from a Chin Track Days weekend at Road Atlanta, advanced solo group. Test days at Polecat run more open track; race weekends add qualifying and the races. The loop is the same everywhere.

  • The night before

    The walkaround, if it is your first time in our car. Belts and seat, the start-up ritual, the sequential gearbox, what the dash is telling you, how we talk on the radio. You do it, we watch. Best done the evening before at the track; if that does not work, plan on a 6:45 arrival instead. It is all in the binder in the trailer if you want to read ahead.

  • 7:30

    You arrive. The car is off the trailer, checked and warmed through. You sign in with the organizer and sit in on the drivers' meeting. If we have already fitted you to the car at the shop, 7:30 is plenty of time; if not, see above. needs Cliff: the drivers'-meeting line is inferred from the 9:00 first session, not sourced

  • 9:00

    Session one, twenty minutes. Out-lap to put temperature in the car, then laps. We are on the radio with flags and time remaining.

  • After every session

    Pressures, checks, video. Tire pressures in pit lane, a nut-and-bolt look at the car, then the video and data with a coach, in plain language. One thing to try next time out.

  • Noon, 1:30, 3:00

    Sessions two to four, same loop. Between sessions the awning is yours: shade, seats, snacks and water, your people.

  • End of day

    The car goes back on the trailer. You go home with the video, and we settle tires and fuel on what you actually used.

Questions

Three questions everyone asks

Do I need a racing license?

Not for a test day or a track day. Time trials need a Time Trials license, a quick online application with no racing school. Wheel-to-wheel racing needs an SCCA competition license or novice permit. The calendar marks every event.

How to get a racing license

What happens if I crash?

The $6,000 crash-damage deposit covers repairs and comes back in full if the car is undamaged. Normal wear is ours, not yours. What a typical crash costs, with examples. needs Cliff: track-day insurance with EPIC to be settled; crash wording needs business and legal review before it ships

What do I bring?
